Two traders take the same trade. One moves the stop to break-even early, and the other leaves it where the plan put it.

Both trades go their way at first, and the first trader wants to protect what he can see, so he pulls the stop up to his entry. Then price pulls back the way it always does before continuing: it touches his entry, closes him for nothing, and carries on without him. The second trader is still in it, because his stop was never placed where the market breathes.

A break-even stop protects your feelings and cancels your trade.
