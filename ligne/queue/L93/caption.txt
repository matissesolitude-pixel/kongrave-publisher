Two traders send an order at the same second, during a news release. One gets the price he asked for, and the other does not.

The first sent a limit order, which names a price and waits: if nobody meets it, nothing happens to him. The second sent a market order, which asks to be filled now, at whatever price is available at that moment — and during a release that price can sit far below the one on his screen. The gap comes straight out of the trade, before the idea has even been judged.

One order names a price. The other accepts one.
